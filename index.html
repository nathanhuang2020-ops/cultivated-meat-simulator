    How the model works

    Each hour, the cells’ growth rate is a best-case maximum multiplied by one “how well is this going?” score between 0 and 1 for each condition. Any single poor score slows everything.

    growth = max rate × nutrients × oxygen × low waste × temperature × free space

    Net change = growth minus death. Death rises when cells starve, suffocate, sit in toxic waste, or overheat.

    What each control does

    • Nutrients: how fast fresh sugars and amino acids are fed in. Cells use them faster as they multiply.
    • Oxygen: how strongly gas is bubbled in. Dense cultures breathe it faster than it dissolves.
    • Temperature: best near 37 °C. Cooler slows division gently; above about 39 °C damage builds quickly.
    • Waste production rate: how fast cells release lactate and ammonia. Waste slows growth, then becomes toxic.

    Assumptions

    • One well-mixed tank and one cell type (muscle precursor cells).
    • Nutrients and oxygen follow saturating (Monod-style) curves.
    • All waste is lumped into one inhibitor.
    • Crowding follows a logistic limit, here set at 20 million cells per mL.
    • Every number is illustrative and was chosen to look plausible, not fitted to data.

    What is left out

    • pH, dissolved CO₂, separate glucose and glutamine, growth factors.
    • Real scaffold chemistry and geometry, and shear stress from stirring.
    • Lag phase, aging, cell-to-cell signalling, and the real molecular control of differentiation.
    • Real reactors need sterile technique, sensors and validated protocols.

    Batch stages in this model

    • Timeline: you pick 14 to 56 days. Stages take fixed shares: inoculation 3%, expansion 32%, scaffold 25%, differentiation 25%, maturation 15%. These shares are invented, not an industry standard. Rates are per hour, so shorter batches reach less differentiation and maturity.
    • Two cell types: precursor cells (P) divide; differentiated cells (D) do not. Once signals start, cells move from P to D at rate k, which falls when nutrients or oxygen are poor: dP/dt = (growth − death − k)P and dD/dt = kP − death × D. Differentiation creates no new cells. Growth is also cut by up to 90% as the differentiated share rises.
    • Medium replacement (manual): the model signals “replace soon” when waste passes 40% or nutrients fall under 15%, and “replace now” at 65% waste or 7% nutrients. Pressing Replace medium removes about 90% of the waste and restores nutrients. These thresholds are invented.
    • Scaffold: microcarrier beads offer a surface for a made-up 14 million cells per mL. Occupancy is attached cells divided by that capacity.
    • Tissue maturity: rises only in the last stage, faster with more attached, differentiated cells in good conditions.

    Real biology vs. invented assumptions

    • Established ideas: precursor cells are multiplied first because differentiated muscle cells mostly stop dividing. Scaffolds and microcarriers give anchorage-dependent cells a surface and structure. Differentiation means specialising into a cell type such as muscle. Maturation organises cells into tissue.
    • Invented here: every duration, rate, percentage, capacity and the 28-day default.
    • Real methods differ a lot: some use scaffolds or microcarriers and some do not, and cell types, media and reactor designs all vary. This model does not predict real production times, yields or manufacturing outcomes.
